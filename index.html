Visual Overview • Minimal Equations

The Geometry
of Integers

On the classical one-dimensional number line, the structural depth of an integer—its step distance from the origin—coincides identically with its scalar magnitude. Confined to a single degree of freedom, integers can translate only forward or backward along a single flat axis.

The Core Concept: Two Degrees of Freedom

By partitioning the integers into horizontal power shells aligned along a central median leaf, every integer acquires two degrees of freedom and generates its own intrinsic two-dimensional coordinates across three canonical directional leaves.

Longitudinal depth along a structural leaf decouples from ambient scalar magnitude—endowing the integers natively with a geodesic metric, multi-valued arithmetic, three prime factorizations per integer, discrete curvature, and prime holography.

This portal presents the visual intuition with minimal equations. We invite you to read the full monograph for complete equations, proofs, and analytical derivations.

Chapter I • Intrinsic 2D Coordinates

Shells & The Four-Way Leaf Intersection

Every integer $n \in \mathbb{N}_m$ sits at the unique four-way intersection of one Horizontal Shell $S_{k,m}$ and three directional Leaves: Left ($\lambda=0$), Vertical ($\lambda=1/2$), and Right ($\lambda=1$).

1. Horizontal Shell ($S_{k,m}$)

Bounded by consecutive $m$-th powers $[k^m, (k+1)^m-1]$, indexed by shell $k = \lfloor\sqrt[m]{n}\rfloor$ and intra-shell residue $r = n - k^m$.

2. Left Leaf ($L_{g_l}, \lambda = 0$)

Tracks constant distance $g_l$ from the left boundary $k^m$, descending from birth shell $k_0(g_l)$ with local depth $d_l = k - k_0(g_l)$.

3. Vertical Leaf ($V_{g_v}, \lambda = 1/2$)

Parallel to the principal vertical leaf $V_{0,m}$: signed offset $g_v$ from the shell median and local depth $d_v = k - k_0(g_v)$.

4. Right Leaf ($R_{g_r}, \lambda = 1$)

Mirror image $\sigma_m(L_g) = R_g$ preserving distance $g_r$ from the right boundary $(k+1)^m-1$, with depth $d_r = k - k_0(g_r)$.

Chapter III • Non-Commutative Triads

Foliated Addition, Subtraction & Multiplication

In $\mathbb{Z}_m$ ($m \ge 2$), addition ($\oplus_m$), subtraction ($\ominus_m$), and multiplication ($\otimes_m$) unfold into directional triads $\lambda \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}$ where $a$ is the geometric leaf-anchor and $b$ is the kinematic driver.

1. Non-Commutative Triads

Advancing along $a$'s leaves by $b$ steps differs from advancing along $b$'s leaves by $a$ steps: $2 \oplus_2 3 = \{17,20,23\} \neq 3 \oplus_2 2 = \{11,13,15\}$, and commutative products $(a \otimes_m b) \cap (b \otimes_m a) = \emptyset$ are always disjoint.

2. The Uniqueness of Prime 31

Across the entire infinite hierarchy $m \ge 2$ and all $2 \le a < b$, the prime $31 \in S_{5,2}$ is the unique cross-direction commutative additive intersection: $(3 \oplus_2 4) \cap (4 \oplus_2 3) = \{\mathbf{31}\}$.

3. Subtraction & Origin Bounce

On the reflected double-lattice $\mathbb{Z}_m$, whenever an inward subtraction step exceeds a leaf's local depth $d_{\lambda,m}(|a|)$, the trajectory bounces across the origin $0$ into the opposite half-lattice $\mathbb{Z}_m^{-\varepsilon_a}$.

Chapter IV • Leaf-Local Arithmetic

Three Prime Factorizations per Integer & Goldbach Coupling

Measuring divisibility by intrinsic leaf depth $d_{\lambda,m}(n)$ endows every individual leaf with an autonomous copy of the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

1. Leaf-Local Unique Factorization

Along any fixed leaf, the local depth map is isomorphic to $(\mathbb{N}^*, \cdot)$. An integer $q$ is a Local Leaf-Depth Prime ($q \in \mathcal{D}_{\lambda,m}$) whenever its intrinsic leaf depth $d_{\lambda,m}(q)$ is prime, giving each integer 3 distinct canonical factorizations across $\lambda \in \{0, 1/2, 1\}$.

2. Goldbach Depth Coupling in $\mathbb{N}_2$

In $\mathbb{N}_2$, the Left and Right local depths sum to $k$ (for $r$ even) or $k-1$ (for $r$ odd). An integer $x \in S_{k,2}$ is simultaneously a Left and Right leaf-depth prime iff $(d_{0,2}(x), d_{1,2}(x))$ forms a Goldbach two-prime partition of $k$ or $k-1$.

3. Minimal Triple Prime $x = 30$

Because $\min(d_{0,2}, d_{1,2}) = \lfloor d_{1/2,2} / 2 \rfloor$, a prime minimum $\ge 2$ forces $d_{1/2,2} \ge 5$, ruling out $(2,2,2)$. The minimal Triple Leaf-Depth Prime in $\mathbb{N}_2$ is the central vertical leaf integer $x = 30 \in S_{5,2}$ with depths $(2, 5, 2)$.

The $m = 1$ Peano Correspondence Principle

At the degenerate boundary $m=1$, lateral generators, discrete holonomy, and curvature vanish identically; the integers lose their second degree of freedom and classical Peano arithmetic is restored.

Section 6.3 • Open Horizons

Four Foundational Questions Beyond the Flat Line

Q1. Foliated Abstract Algebra

What is the natural axiomatization of a "foliated ring" or "foliated module" when single-valued commutativity is replaced by directional triads and holonomy commutators?

Q2. The Foliated Real Continuum

In the continuum limit of $\mathbb{Z}_m$, how do discrete transport operators transition into directional covariant derivatives and continuous curvature two-forms on $\mathbb{R}$?

Q3. Native Complex Phase from Cones

Since circular periodicity is already native to the closed conical shells of $\mathbb{Z}_2$, can $\mathbb{C}$ emerge directly from helical transport without adjoining an extrinsic $i = \sqrt{-1}$?

Q4. Quaternionic $(1+3)$ Self-Shift Triad

Does the native $(1+3)$ operational structure of $\mathbb{Z}_m$ (1 shell depth + 3 non-commuting self-shift operators across Left, Vertical, and Right leaves) provide an arithmetic precursor to Hamilton's quaternions $\mathbb{H}$?
